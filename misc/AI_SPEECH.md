# AI Speech: A Guide to Spotting Artificial Language Patterns

AI speech is recognizable by specific patterns and structures that make writing sound polished but hollow. Here are the markers:

## Contrast Structures
- "Not X but Y" - Creates false binary choices
- "While X, also Y" - Hedging both sides simultaneously
- "Not because A, but because B" - Softening statements with qualifications
- "On one hand... on the other hand" - Performing balance instead of taking a stance

## Hyphenated Constructions
- Using em dashes to add dramatic pauses: "The answer was clear—he had failed"
- Hyphens to connect concepts artificially: "ground-level freedom" instead of just saying it

## Qualification Language
- "It could be argued that..."
- "In some sense..."
- "To a certain extent..."
- "One might say..."
- "It seems that..."

## Hedging Phrases
- "I may be wrong, but..."
- "I'm not a lawyer, but..."
- "That said..."
- "To be fair..."
- "In fairness..."

## False Depth Markers
- "The thing about X is..." - Suggests wisdom without delivering it
- "What this really means is..." - Performing insight
- "At its core..." - Artificial excavation
- "The truth is..." - Claiming authority without earning it

## Linking Clichés
- "In other words..."
- "Put another way..."
- "What I'm trying to say is..."
- "The point being..."

## Softening Endings
- Adding a qualifier at the end to take back what was just said
- Ending strong statements with hesitation
- Following conviction with doubt

## Over-Structure
- Breaking thoughts into numbered or bulleted lists when simple sentences would work
- Using headers for single paragraphs
- Creating false organization to appear comprehensive

## Abstract Language When Concrete Would Work
- "Demonstrates a propensity toward..." instead of "tends to"
- "Facilitate the process of..." instead of "help with"
- "In the context of..." instead of "in"

## Observation Verbs That Sound Detached
- "I can see..."
- "It appears that..."
- "One notices..."
- "It could be observed that..."

## The Setup-Payoff Structure
- "The interesting thing about this is..."
- "What's important to understand here is..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- Then delivering something obvious

## Acknowledgment of Limitation as a Move
- "I don't know, but..."
- "I'm not sure, however..."
- Using uncertainty as a rhetorical device rather than actual uncertainty

---

**What to listen for:**
If it sounds like someone is performing understanding rather than actually saying something, it's AI speech. If there are escape routes built into every sentence, that's AI speech. If the language is too perfect or too structured, that's AI speech.

Real human speech is messier. It commits. It doesn't hedge. It says what it means.
