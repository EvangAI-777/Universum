## 1. Author Preface

Throughout my life, I kept getting asked the same question: how was I able to be creative and technically minded at the same time? People always wondered how I could combine things that looked, to them, like they belonged in separate rooms. This book is the answer to that lifelong question.

- **The question that started it.** The recurring puzzle others handed me — the creative mind and the technical mind, held together in one person — and why answering it required a whole book rather than a sentence.
- **Background about the author.** Who I am, where the thought process was formed, and the path that made this integration natural rather than forced.

## 2. Introduction

### Overview of the book's concepts

So, why choose *Currents* as the title? When we talk about currents, we usually mean electricity or water. But I wanted a much larger scope — a scope that follows life's *general tendency*, its course of events. A current is a single motion that carries many things at once. That is what this book is about: the one movement running through every subject we usually keep apart.

- **The central image.** Current as the thing that connects — not a metaphor laid on top of the subjects, but the actual continuity between them.
- **The claim.** That the fields we treat as separate are sections of one moving thing, and that seeing the connection is the point.

### How to read the book

- **On the structure.** The book moves from *Natural Life* to *Eternal Life*, and the middle is the bridge. Each subject emerges out of the one before it — the transitions are where the coherence lives.
- **On "by way of."** Throughout, one field is reached *by way of* another (music by way of notation, math by way of music, and so on). These are not detours. They are the current itself, made visible at the seams.

## 3. Natural Life

The physical world, taken not as dead matter but as the near bank of a single stream — the place where the current is most visible to the senses.

### 3.1 Science

#### Psychology
The inner life as a natural phenomenon — mind studied as part of the world rather than apart from it.

#### Geology
- **The Earth as a living being.** The planet considered not as inert rock but as something with its own life and motion.
- **Animate and inanimate.** The nature of the line between living and non-living things — and how firm that line actually is.

#### Chemistry
- **States of matter.** Solid, liquid, gas, and the transitions between them as the simplest picture of one substance moving through forms.
- **Chemicals.** The building blocks and their combinations.

#### Physics
- **Particle physics and quantum mechanics.** The smallest scale, where matter stops behaving like the solid world we assumed and starts behaving like something stranger and more fluid.

#### Biology
- **Humans.**
  - The body, soul, and spirit working together to form the whole person — refusing the split between the material and the meaningful.
  - The eyes and other body parts as "windows to the soul": the physical form opening onto the inner one.
- **Animals.** Life in its other forms, and what they share with us.
- **Plants.** Life without a nervous system, growing and reaching all the same.
- **Insects.** Life at the small scale, organized in ways that look almost designed.

### 3.2 Computer Science
*(reached by way of electrical signals in the brain: neurons and synapses)*

The brain runs on electrical signals across neurons and synapses — which means thought itself has a current running through it. From there, the leap to machines is short.

- **Coding.** Instructions given in a language a system can execute.
- **Algorithms.** Ordered procedures — the logic of a process written down.
- **Problem-solving.** The underlying skill the machine only imitates: breaking a thing down until it moves.

### 3.3 Music
*(reached by way of musical notation being the first coding language)*

Before there were programming languages, there was musical notation — a written system of symbols that, when executed, produces a precise result in time. The first code.

- **Music cryptography.** Hidden structure and encoded meaning within musical notation.
- **Music as the original programming language.** Notation as a full instruction set — the case that composition and coding are the same act in different symbols.

### 3.4 Math
*(reached by way of harmonic series, amplitudes, and sine waves being used in music)*

Music turns out to be made of math — the harmonic series, amplitudes, sine waves. Pull that thread and the whole of mathematics comes with it.

- **Arithmetic.** The ground floor: quantity and its operations.
- **Algebra.** Relationships between unknowns; structure abstracted from specific numbers.
- **Geometry.**
  - **Sacred geometry / shape power.** The idea that certain forms carry meaning, not just measurement.
  - **Trigonometry.** The math of angles and waves — the direct link back to sound.
- **Statistics.** Pattern drawn out of many instances.
- **Calculus.** The mathematics of change and motion itself — current in its most literal mathematical form.

### 3.5 Communication and Language
*(reached by way of math being converted into various language structures)*

Math is itself a language — the language of science. Once you see that, language in general opens up as the next section of the current: structure carrying meaning from one mind to another.

- **Math as the language of science.** The most precise human language, and the model for the rest.
- **Methods of communication.** Writing, speaking, reading — the channels through which structure moves between people.

#### Human Languages
- **Verbal communication (natural languages).**
  - *Standard dialect* — the variety used in public discourse and social institutions.
  - *Non-standard dialect* — vernacular and slang; language that never received institutional sanction but carries its own full life.
  - *The five love languages* — the ways affection itself is spoken.
  - *Fictional languages* — invented tongues spoken by humans and other races of creatures.
- **Non-verbal communication.**
  - Body language.
  - Sign language.
  - The five love languages, again — because much of love is spoken without words.
- **Formal languages (spoken by computers).** High-level and low-level languages — the loop back to Section 3.2.

#### Animal Languages and Insect Communication
- **Forms of animal communication.** The signals, calls, and gestures animals use.
- **Human–animal communication.**
  - The story of Balaam and his donkey — Scripture's own account of an animal given speech.
  - Animal whisperers.
  - Animals learning human language and responding to it.
- **Fictional animal languages.**
  - *Watership Down* by Richard Adams.
  - *The Plague Dogs* by Richard Adams.
  - Animal narratology as a field.
- **Insect communication.** The known techniques — chemical, vibrational, and otherwise.

#### Heavenly Languages
- **Angelic languages.** The speech of higher orders.
- **Demonic languages.** Their counterpart.

### 3.6 Literature

Literature is where the full extent of human experience gets carried in words — the current running through story.

- **We are characters in a larger story.** Just as a book has a protagonist supported by side characters, we can learn to be effective main characters in our own lives and effective side characters in the lives of others.
  - The nature and role of the *protagonist* (main character).
  - The nature and role of the *deuteragonist* (side character).
  - How to apply these principles in our own lives.
- **Literature as intersection.** The meeting point of human experience and divine powers, feelings, and influences.
- **Literature as lens.** A way of seeing both the spiritual and physical worlds at once.

### 3.7 Law (Legal Analysis)

- **Law throughout the world.**
  - Human constitutions, compared and contrasted.
  - The laws of nature — order written into the physical world itself.
- **Law in Scripture.**
  - *The Law of Moses*, including the Ten Commandments.
  - *The Law of the Spirit* — love and grace as the fulfillment of the commandments: love God with all your heart, soul, and mind; love your neighbor as yourself.

### 3.8 History

History as the scientific way of organizing world events — the current of time itself, ordered so it can be read. *(Much to be developed here.)*
