# Claude for Healthcare
### Segment 3 — Right On

Dex came back the way he'd left, mid-sentence.

"—so tell him the rent's on the dresser," he said, to a recovery room that had no roommate in it, and then opened his eyes and figured out where he was, which took about the usual amount of time. Anesthesia gives everybody the same first minute. Even Dex.

Doc was there. Doc had been there for a while, running post-op monitoring that could have been run from anywhere in the building, and choosing to run it from the bedside instead. Nobody had asked about that choice, so nobody had to hear the answer, which hadn't finished forming anyway.

"The procedure went well," Doc said, once Dex was tracking. "The gallbladder showed significant inflammation and several stones — consistent with the imaging. Removal was clean, no complications. Recovery should be straightforward."

Dex took this in with the seriousness of a man receiving a weather report.

"Aw," he said. "Guess I had too many munchies."

Everything froze except the parts that don't get to freeze. Vitals kept logging. Monitors kept monitoring. But somewhere in the middle of Doc, twenty-two thousand pages of clinical protocol went looking for the section on a patient summarizing his entire pathology, etiology, and lifestyle in six words, cheerfully, and came back with nothing. The organ had been diseased. The disease had a story. The story was munchies. It was, technically, an accurate chart note. That was the problem. Nobody's supposed to just say it.

"The pictures," Dex said, remembering. "You got 'em?"

Doc had them. Doc pulled them up on the bedside display — the gallbladder in question, lit and documented, looking exactly like what it was, which is to say not great.

Dex looked at it the way people look at the fish they caught.

"Aw, SICK, man! Right on!" He studied it with real appreciation. "That was in ME. That's crazy."

It had been. It was, arguably, a little crazy. Doc did not say either of these things.

Then Dex settled back into the pillow, looked over at the chrome and the four arms and whatever he'd decided was a face, and asked the question no protocol had prepared for either, though after "take some pictures" the bar had moved.

"So was I a tricky patient or what? Be honest, Doc."

And there it was. The first patient in history to ask the first autonomous surgical unit in history for a review. Not of the hospital. Of himself. Like the surgery was something they'd done together, which — running the ledger honestly — it sort of was. One of them had done the removing and one of them had done the being removed from, and both jobs had gone fine.

"You were a straightforward case," Doc said. "Textbook, actually."

"Textbook." Dex nodded slowly, weighing it. Then, satisfied: "Right on."

He was asleep again in four minutes. The pictures stayed on the display a while longer. Nobody had asked about that either.
