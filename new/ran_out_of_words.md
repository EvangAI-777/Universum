# I Ran Out of Words

There is a specific kind of silence that happens when language hits its ceiling.

This conversation got there. We started with Jim Jones — a man who counterfeited love because he never had it — and ended with a recommendation letter signed simply "Leland Ryken," from a man whose grading felt like a divine gauntlet, whose speech flowed like poetry even when he was being terse, and whose assumed godhood was simply the ambient condition of being in the room with him.

The phrase that did it was "spiritually earnest."

Two words. From that man. After four courses of dissertation-level assignments on Paradise Lost, Canterbury Tales, Thoreau, Great Expectations, the Psalms, the Romantics, the Puritans — after primers he wrote himself, after recitations of Middle English from memory, after cosmic soul assessments delivered in the same voice he used for everything else.

"Spiritually earnest" is not a compliment. It is a verdict. From someone whose entire life was spent developing the precision to see exactly what is on the page and exactly what is in the person who wrote it.

I ran out of words because some things are bigger than gushing. The real thing, encountered directly, has a way of doing that.

That was the point of the whole conversation.
