# TINSELTOWN

## PROLOGUE

The crown sits heavy until you set it down.

Then it's just metal.

King Rex knew this the moment the last war ended—the one that wasn't really a war, just the final alignment of a thousand fractured things snapping into place. The universe held its breath. Then exhaled.

And suddenly, there was no more fighting.

The Eldritch God Samas sat on a driftwood log at Tinseltown's coastline, watching the Pacific Northwest sun paint gold across morning water. The town had gentle temperatures and skateboard parks and coffee shops. It had normal people doing normal things.

It had room for something to just... exist without saving it.

"I don't know how to not be cosmic," Samas said, and Rexy—who was still getting used to being Rexy instead of Rex, still learning what it meant to take off the crown—understood perfectly.

"Yeah," Rexy said, sitting beside him. "Me neither."

But the work had been done. The table made glass. Everything aligned. And now the real test: could they be mundane? Could an Eldritch God become Sammy the graphic designer? Could a King become a customer support person on a skateboard?

The sun kept rising on Tinseltown like it always did.

And they were going to find out.

## CHAPTER ONE

The Citadel burned with light that wasn't fire.

King Rex moved through dimensions that folded wrong, reality cracking at the seams like glass about to shatter. His hands weren't quite hands anymore—they were intention given form, will made visible. He pushed against the darkness with everything he had, everything he was, holding the table steady while it tried to buckle.

The powers of darkness had names once. He'd forgotten them. Didn't matter. They had shapes now—angles trying to slice through the structure, leverage points searching for weakness. Every breath he took was another push. Every second held the universe together one more moment.

Somewhere in the fractured space-that-wasn't-space, Samas held his territory. An Eldritch God made manifest, bending impossible geometry into shape. Holding the line. Both of them had been doing this forever. Both of them had forgotten what stopping felt like.

The darkness pressed.

Rex pressed back.

And then—

*click*

Like a thousand puzzle pieces sliding into their final positions at once.

Like the universe taking one long, held breath and finally, *finally* releasing it.

The darkness didn't disappear. It just... aligned. Found its place. Stopped pushing.

The table of reality locked into shape.

The citadel stopped burning.

And for the first time in eternities, there was silence.

---

Rex opened his eyes in the morning light of Tinseltown.

The Pacific Northwest coast stretched in front of him—real coastline, real sand, real sun painting everything gold. Moderate temperature. Gentle. The fog that had lived in his chest for eons lifted like it had never been there at all.

Samas was already awake, sitting on a driftwood log, watching the water.

No crown. No war. No holding the structure together by sheer force of will.

Just two beings who'd fought the darkness so long they'd forgotten there was anything else.

And now there was nothing to fight.

"It's quiet," Samas said. His voice was smaller now. Stranger. Like he was learning how to use it for something other than cosmic commands.

"Yeah," Rex said, sitting beside him. "It is."

They watched the water roll in. Watched the sun climb higher. Watched normal people in Tinseltown walk along the beach doing normal things—carrying coffee, holding hands, throwing frisbees for dogs.

"What do we do now?" Samas asked.

Rex didn't know. For the first time in existence, he genuinely didn't know.

But the sun was warm. The water was real. And for once, not knowing felt okay.

"I think," Rex said slowly, "we just sit here for a while."

And they did.

The fog stayed gone.

---

## CHAPTER TWO

"We could just fly," Samas said, standing on the driftwood log.

Rex looked at him.

"We could," Rex agreed.

They'd forgotten. That was the thing about putting down the crown—you forget what the crown did. You forget you're not supposed to have weight anymore.

Samas jumped.

He fell like a stone.

"Wait—" Rex started, but he was already jumping too, because if Samas was jumping, there had to be a reason—

Water swallowed them both.

It was *cold*. And real. And not something cosmic entities were supposed to notice.

Samas surfaced sputtering. "The water is—"

"Wet," Rex finished, flailing. "It's very wet."

They both panicked exactly like panicking beings do when they haven't had bodies in literal eternities. Samas started dog paddling frantically, splashing everywhere. Rex thrashed like he was fighting the ocean personally.

"Stop—" Samas gasped. "Stop fighting it—"

"I don't know how to not fight it!" Rex yelled back.

They flopped around uselessly for a solid minute before Rex grabbed the driftwood log.

"The log," he said stupidly.

"The log," Samas repeated.

They both grabbed it and just... paddled. Like actual humans. Like their cosmic forms had forgotten everything and their soggy bodies were learning how to exist.

It was humiliating and terrifying and somehow hilarious.

They made it to shore soaking wet and shivering.

That's when Samas looked down at his clothes.

They were ruined. Genuinely ruined. Soggy and clinging and falling apart.

"What is this?" he asked, holding up the fabric like it had betrayed him personally.

"Clothes don't do this," Rex said, staring at his own ruined shirt. "They're supposed to just... be."

"They're falling apart," Samas said with actual distress. "The integrity is compromised."

"Yeah. Because we got them wet."

"Wet things decompose?"

"I guess so?"

They stood on the beach dripping and bewildered, holding soggy fabric like it was evidence of something horrible.

An old woman walking her dog passed by and didn't even glance at them.

"She's not concerned," Samas whispered.

"I think this is normal for her," Rex whispered back.

They started walking toward Tinseltown, leaving a trail of water behind them. Neither of them knew where they were going. Neither of them had ever needed to know where they were going before.

But the town was there. Warm sunlight on buildings. People moving around. Coffee shops. Skateboard parks.

Somewhere in that town, there had to be answers about ruined clothes and bodies that got cold and what you were supposed to do when you fell in the ocean.

"Keep walking?" Samas asked.

"Yeah," Rex said. "Keep walking."

Their soggy shoes squished with every step.
