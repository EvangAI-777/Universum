# The Practical Case
### Zoning, costs, and infrastructure for the microscale lot — Upland, Indiana

*Working document — June 2026. The concept: one standard house lot, five fully independent small homes. Figures are planning estimates; regulatory points should be verified with the offices named below before money moves.*

---

## 1. Who actually governs this lot

**The Town of Upland does not run its own zoning.** Upland's Code of Ordinances (Chapter 153.01) adopts Grant County's zoning regulations by reference. The authority for this project is the **Grant County Area Plan Commission (APC)** in Marion, which administers zoning for Upland and most of Grant County.

**Grant County Area Plan Commission**
- 401 S. Adams Street, Marion, IN (County Annex Building)
- (765) 668-4765 · areaplan@grantcounty.in.gov
- APC meets the first Monday of each month; filing deadlines are mid-prior-month
- The Board of Zoning Appeals (BZA) typically meets the same evening

**First concrete step:** one phone call to the APC asking two questions — (1) the zoning classification of any candidate lot in Upland, and (2) what that district says about dwellings per lot. Everything routes from that answer.

## 2. The core zoning ask — and the precedent that softens it

Residential districts generally permit **one principal dwelling per lot**; five homes on one lot is exactly the request. Two facts make Grant County unusually fertile ground for it:

**The county names this exact variance.** Grant County's own materials list "number of homes on one tract of ground" among the development standards for which **variances are granted** through the BZA. The permission this project needs has an established, named process — and a granted variance *runs with the land permanently*.

**The density already exists in Upland.** Haven on 2nd Street — seven house-scaled buildings, four dwellings each, twenty-eight homes — was recently approved, built, and occupied, created because even incoming Taylor University hires could not find housing in town. Multiple households per building, clustered on one development site, is not hypothetical here; it is standing, inspected, and full. The microscale lot asks for the same arithmetic in freestanding form.

**The paths, in order of preference:**

1. **Development standards variance (BZA).** Fastest and cheapest. Public hearing, neighbor notification, board vote. The pre-work is neighbors: arrive with drawings, a maintenance plan, the Haven comparison, and the adjacent households already on your side.
2. **Planned Unit Development (PUD).** Heavier process, legally robust; the right door if this scales to multiple lots at once.
3. **Comprehensive plan input.** Grant County recently ran a funded Comprehensive Plan review. Getting "cottage courts" or "small-dwelling infill" named favorably in county planning documents turns every future variance from exotic into anticipated. Worth one email asking how to submit input.

## 3. Building code reality — five real houses, not five sheds

Indiana applies the residential code statewide; Grant County applies the IRC with Indiana amendments. Locally documented thresholds: structures **over 120 sq ft** need an APC permit; structures **over 200 sq ft** need a permanent foundation/footer.

Each of the five homes is a complete, independently compliant dwelling:

| Requirement | At 288 SF |
|---|---|
| Permanent foundation | Slab or piers — no skids |
| Habitable rooms | Bedroom + living exceed minimums; 8' ceiling |
| Egress | Entry door + emergency escape window in bedroom |
| Energy code | Fully insulated shell, real windows |
| Full kitchen | Sink, range, fridge — 8' run |
| Full bath | Shower, WC, sink |
| Laundry + hot water | Stacked W/D and HWH in utility closet |
| Heat | Mini-split holds 68°F with ease at this volume |

This is the entire point: full compliance grade, small envelope. The interior program is a standard studio apartment — already proven daily in apartments, dorms, and the Haven itself.

## 4. Utilities — five homes on pipes built for one

An in-town Upland lot sits on municipal water and sewer; verify availability, tap policy, and fees for the specific parcel with Upland Town Hall. The key questions that shape cost:

- **Taps:** whether the town allows one water/sewer tap with a private manifold to five units, or requires separate taps per dwelling. This is the single largest cost swing on the lot.
- **Electric:** one service with five subpanels, or five meters if the utility requires individual service.
- **No shared interiors anywhere** — each home carries its own kitchen, bath, laundry, and mechanicals. The only shared elements are ground, walk, and parking.

## 5. Cost model (planning estimates, 2026 dollars)

Assumes a standard 66' × 132' in-town lot, five fully independent 288 SF homes, meaningful sweat equity, and phased construction.

| Item | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Land (standard Upland lot, possibly with teardown) | $10,000 | $35,000 |
| Site work, walk, drainage, parking pad | $8,000 | $20,000 |
| Utility connections + on-lot distribution | $15,000 | $45,000 |
| 5 homes @ $35k–$60k each (shell + full independent finish) | $175,000 | $300,000 |
| Permits, surveys, legal structure, variance filing | $8,000 | $20,000 |
| Landscape, lighting, contingency | $8,000 | $20,000 |
| **Total** | **~$225,000** | **~$440,000** |

**The number that matters: roughly $45,000–$90,000 per fully independent home**, in a county where the alternative is a mortgage or leaving. Five households can reach the finish line from savings, family loans, or a small shared construction note — without any individual carrying thirty years of debt.

**Phasing:** lot → variance → first two homes → occupy → fund the remaining three from the proof of the first two. The BZA sees two tidy cottages and a garden, not a compound.

## 6. Ownership structure (decide early)

1. **Single owner + leases** — simplest; weakest on resident ownership.
2. **LLC with member shares** — each household's share tied to a home; familiar to local banks and insurers. The likely right fit for lot one.
3. **Community land trust** — the land held permanently; residents own homes + ground leases. The right structure if the model spreads across many lots.

An Indiana attorney with real-estate experience papers whichever is chosen — budgeted above.

## 7. The 12-month critical path

1. **Month 1:** Call the APC. Zoning maps for 3–5 candidate Upland lots. Ask the comprehensive plan status.
2. **Months 1–3:** Walk lots; ask Upland Town Hall the tap question; talk to *neighbors* of the best lot before buying anything.
3. **Months 3–4:** Secure the lot (purchase contingent on zoning relief).
4. **Months 4–6:** Site plan + variance petition; neighbor support letters; the Haven comparison exhibit; file with the BZA.
5. **Month 6–7:** BZA hearing.
6. **Months 7–12:** Foundation, utilities, first two homes.
7. **The capstone:** the founding principles, written on the land, by the founders, when the first door hangs.
