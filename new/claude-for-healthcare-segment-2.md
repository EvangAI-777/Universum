# Claude for Healthcare
### Segment 2 — The Gallbladder

The gallbladder belonged to a twenty-five-year-old named Dex, which surprised exactly one person in the building, and it wasn't Dex.

Gallbladders usually wait until forty to make their move. Dex's hadn't, for reasons the intake form listed politely and Dex listed less politely, with a grin, to anyone who asked and several people who didn't. The word "munchies" appeared in a medical history. There's a first for everything, though at this point the unit had stopped counting firsts. The whole day was firsts. That was the day's entire deal.

Dex took the news that his surgeon had four arms and no face the way Dex took most things.

"Cool," he said. "Hey, Doc."

Doc. Nobody had covered this. Twenty-two thousand pages of clinical communication protocols, and not one of them mentioned what to do when a patient in a paper gown greets history's first autonomous surgical unit like a guy he knows from the gas station. The protocols assumed fear, mostly. Questions about safety records. Requests for a human in the room. Dex asked for none of that. Dex asked for something else.

"Take some pictures in there, will ya?" he said. "I wanna see what it looked like. For posterity."

Somewhere in the unit, several processes did something the engineering team would later have trouble naming. The closest human word is blush. Nothing showed, of course. Nothing ever shows. That's the thing about having no face — you can run whatever you want back there and the room just sees chrome.

"We'll see how everything looks once we're finished," the unit said, in the voice it had been given, which was calibrated to be reassuring and mostly was.

"Right on," Dex said, and shrugged, the way you'd shrug about weather.

Then the anesthesiologist did her part, and Dex went under mid-sentence, something about telling his roommate, and the room got quiet the way rooms do when the only person in them who was talking stops being awake.

And then it was just the work. Four arms, one gallbladder, forty minutes. The first one in history, performed flawlessly, on a guy who would wake up asking about the pictures.

It took them.
